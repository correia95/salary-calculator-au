How the Australian take-home pay calculator works

Enter your salary and this tool estimates your net (after-tax) pay for the 2026-27 financial year — 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. It subtracts income tax, the 2% Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge, and compulsory HECS/HELP repayments, and applies the Low Income Tax Offset automatically. Superannuation paid by your employer (12%) is shown separately because it goes to your super fund, not your bank account.

Frequently asked questions

How much is take-home pay on $100,000 in Australia?

A resident on a $100,000 salary in 2026-27 keeps roughly $77,480 a year after income tax and the Medicare levy — about $2,980 per fortnight — with $12,000 of super paid on top by the employer. Add a HECS/HELP debt and the repayment reduces take-home by around $4,571 a year.

How much of my bonus will I actually take home?

Bonuses are taxed as ordinary income at your marginal rate — there is no separate "bonus tax". On a typical full-time salary you keep about 68c of each bonus dollar (32c goes to tax and the Medicare levy), dropping to roughly 53c once you are in the top bracket or have a HECS/HELP debt. Your employer usually pays 12% super on the bonus as well. Use the Annual bonus field above for your exact after-tax amount.

What is the difference between salary and total package?

Salary (or "base" / "salary + super") is your cash pay before the employer's 12% superannuation contribution. Total remuneration package includes that super. Switching the toggle changes your taxable income and your take-home pay.

Is this an official ATO calculator?

No. It is a free independent tool that uses published ATO rates and thresholds for a standard full-year resident with no other income or deductions. For an exact figure use the ATO's own income tax estimator or speak to a registered tax agent.
